F1 TERMINOLOGY

What does "Boxing" mean?

In Formula 1, when an engineer radios "Box, box, box!", they are instructing the driver to enter the pit lane immediately for a pit stop. The term comes from the German word Boxenstopp and refers to the team's designated "pit box" area where 20 mechanics change all 4 wheels in under 2.5 seconds. In TrackLab, deciding which lap you box determines whether you win or lose.

02 // Tires Explained Simply

Speed vs. Durability.

In F1, tires are called compounds. Soft tires give you the fastest speed and cornering grip, but wear out quickly. Hard tires are slightly slower, but last for dozens of laps. Medium tires give you a balanced blend of both.

What is "The Cliff"? As tires wear down, grip drops slowly at first. But when rubber overheats too much, you hit the cliff—where lap times suddenly become 2+ seconds slower and you are forced to make a pit stop.

03 // Pit Stop Tactics

The art of the pit stop.

A pit stop costs roughly 18.4 seconds at Norisring. Winning races is all about knowing the exact lap to come in:

🛑 "Box, Box" = Pit Stop The team radio instruction telling the driver to enter the pit lane to change tires right now.
⚡ The Undercut (Pit Early) Pitting a lap or two before your rival. Fresh tires are so fast that when your rival pits later, you've already jumped ahead of them.
🔄 The Overcut (Pit Late) Staying out on track while your rival pits. If your car is light and your old tires still have grip, you race in clean air to build a lead.
