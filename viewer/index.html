Part 1 // Educational Briefing

Understanding Tyre Compounds

Teams choose between 3 dry slick compounds. Softer rubber heats up quickly and provides more grip in corners, but wears down rapidly over consecutive laps.

Soft Compound (C3)
Fastest initial pace. High thermal degradation.
PACE: BASE (0.0s)
LIFE: ~14–18 LAPS
Medium Compound (C2)
Balanced pace and durability across medium stints.
PACE: +0.45s / LAP
LIFE: ~22–30 LAPS
Hard Compound (C1)
Slowest warm-up and peak grip, lowest wear rate.
PACE: +0.85s / LAP
LIFE: ~32–45 LAPS
FIA Sporting Regulation: Every driver must use at least 2 different dry compounds during a dry race.

Part 2 // Educational Briefing

Pit Stop Delta & The Degradation Cliff

Every pit stop costs roughly 21 seconds driving down the pit lane. You only win that time back if your old tyres are losing significant time compared to fresh rubber.

01 // THE WEAR CLIFF
Pitting Too Late

Once a tyre passes its optimal lap window, grip drops non-linearly ("hitting the cliff"), costing 1.5s to 2.5s per lap.

02 // FUEL WEIGHT EFFECT
Fuel Burn Advantage

Cars burn ~1.8kg of fuel per lap. Every 10kg burned makes the car ~0.3s faster per lap as the race progresses.
