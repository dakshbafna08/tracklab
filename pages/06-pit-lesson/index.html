Part 01 // Pit Wall Tactics

Box, Undercut & Overcut

How teams use pit lane timing to overtake rivals without passing on track.

RADIO COMMAND: "BOX, BOX, BOX!"

Why do engineers say "Box"?

Originating from the German word Boxenstopp, "Box" refers to the team's pit garage stall. When the race engineer commands "Box, box!", the driver must immediately enter the pit lane on that lap for 4 fresh tires.

Strategy #1 Pit Earlier

The Undercut

"Pit 1–2 laps before the car ahead of you."

Stuck behind a rival in dirty air? You pit first for brand-new tires. While your rival remains on worn rubber, your fresh grip allows you to set an ultra-fast out-lap. When they pit on the next lap, you emerge ahead!

Strategy #2 Pit Later

The Overcut

"Stay out on track while your rival pits."

Your rival pits early. Instead of covering immediately, you stay out with an empty track ahead. If your tires still have life, you pump in fast laps without traffic and pit a few laps later, exiting cleanly ahead.

The Three Dry Compounds

A stint is simply the continuous segment of laps driven on one set of tires.

Soft (Red)

Maximum launch grip off the grid and explosive qualifying pace. The softest compound grips asphalt instantly but burns out quickly.

Mandatory Rule

The FIA Two-Compound Mandate

In dry track conditions, Formula 1 Sporting Regulations require every driver to use at least two different tire compounds during the race (e.g. starting on Soft, then switching to Medium or Hard). You cannot drive the entire race on one compound—even if the tires never wear out.
