F1 TRACKLAB
04 // Pit Stops Made Simple

What are "Box",
"Undercut" & "Overcut"?

In F1, races aren't just won by passing on track. Teams use pit stop timing to jump ahead of rivals while they're in the pit lane. Here is how it works in plain English:

Radio Command

Why do teams say "Box, Box"?

"Box" simply means "Make a pit stop right now." It originates from the German word Boxenstopp and refers to the team's pit garage (box). When a driver's engineer yells "Box, box, box!" over the radio, the driver must dive into the pit lane on that lap to change tires.

Strategy #1 Pit Earlier

The Undercut

"Pit 1–2 laps before the car ahead of you."

You are stuck behind a rival and can't pass. You pit first for brand-new, ultra-grippy tires.

✓ How you pass them:

While your rival is still circulating on old, worn-out rubber, you drive a blistering fast lap on brand-new tires. When they finally pit a lap later, you’ve already sped past them down the straight!

Strategy #2 Pit Later

The Overcut

"Stay out on track while your rival pits."

Your rival pits ahead of you. Instead of pitting immediately, you stay out on track.

✓ How you pass them:

If your old tires still have decent grip and your rival exits the pits into slow traffic, you have empty track (clean air) ahead. You hammer down fast laps with a lighter car and emerge ahead after your pit stop!
